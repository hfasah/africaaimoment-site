# Endorsement outreach — draft blurbs for reviewers

These are **suggested drafts to send with a review copy**, so a busy professor or founder can edit and sign one in two minutes instead of writing from scratch. Nothing here goes on the site or the cover until a named person has approved it in writing. Standard practice: offer the draft, make clear they can rewrite it entirely, and use exactly the words they return.

## The ask (email to send with the PDF or ARC)

**Subject:** Would you consider a short endorsement for Africa's AI Moment?

Hi {{Name}},

I'm publishing *Africa's AI Moment: From Consumers of Technology to Creators of the Future* this fall — 17 chapters on how Africa moves from consuming AI to building, governing, and owning it, with an audit of 10 African economies against the G7 (attached: the audit as a 13-page report, plus the full manuscript).

Your work on {{their area}} shaped how I think about {{specific chapter}}, and a line from you would mean a lot to the readers I wrote it for — students, founders, policymakers, and the diaspora.

If it helps, I've drafted a couple of options below — please rewrite freely or ignore them entirely. One or two sentences is plenty, and I'd use exactly your words with your name and title as you'd like them shown. No pressure on timing, but I'm finalizing the cover in {{month}}.

With thanks,
Hippolyte

## Draft blurbs — for the reviewer to adapt

**Academic / policy voice (2 sentences)**
> "Asah does something rare: he puts numbers on Africa's AI gap and then refuses to leave it there. The BUILD framework turns a diagnosis into a plan that ministries, universities, and founders can actually act on."

**Academic voice, alternative**
> "The most useful book I've read on AI and African development — grounded in the IMF and Stanford data, but written for people who have to make decisions on Monday morning."

**Founder / builder voice (2 sentences)**
> "Every African founder should read this before their next fundraise. It explains, plainly, why building on rented intelligence is a trap — and what owning the stack actually requires."

**Founder voice, alternative**
> "Asah has spent twenty years building systems, and it shows. This is a practical book about capability and ownership, not another warning about being left behind."

**Diaspora / leadership voice**
> "For those of us in the diaspora who keep asking how to contribute beyond remittances, this book finally answers: knowledge, networks, and capital — and here is where to put them."

**Educator voice**
> "I'm assigning this. It gives students a framework for thinking about AI as something Africans build, not something that happens to them."

**Short, cover-length (under 15 words)**
> "The clearest case yet for Africa as a creator, not a consumer, of AI."
> "Required reading for anyone who wants Africa to own its AI future."
